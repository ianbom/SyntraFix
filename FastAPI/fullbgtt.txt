Abstract: Digital twin cities integrated road damage inspection to support smart road infrastructure. The road's condition was assessed using road damage survey metrics, and the issue was resolved manually. Realtime, automatically, and precisely employing artificial intelligence (AI) is needed to improve road safety and reduce maintenance costs. This research on road defect detection utilizes improved YOLOV7 (iYOLOV7) while multilevel hyperparameter optimization using combination of the Tree-Structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) and Search Space (SS). This model we call TPE-SS. The iYOLOV7 provides real-time processing on edge devices that have limited storage, low-speed processors, and memory. The research investigates how leveraging multilevel hyperparameter optimization using the TPE-SS model improves system performance where road damage is detected. The results of iYOLOV7 model demonstrate the smallest total loss of 0.1, indicating a significant performance improvement where a precision value of 0.986, an average recall (AR) of 0.970

In the literature, road data acquisition refers to the process of gathering road surface condition data using cameras and deep learning to identify instances of road damage, also known as the difficulty of road damage detection. Many deep learningbased techniques, including CNN models, have been utilized in the literature to address the issues of recognizing road defects. Some researchers utilize manual hyperparameter tuning and optimization strategies to ensure that the CNN model performs optimally.

Maeda et al. [33] utilized the SSD-InceptionV2 and SSD-MobileNet models. The dataset collection includes bounding boxes for each of eight types of road damage. The data collection includes the bounding boxes for each of the eight forms of road damage. Each image is derived from an image set that was made by taking images of a large number of roads with a vehicle-mounted smartphone. The SSD-InceptionV2 and SSD-MobileNet model frameworks were loaded on a smartphone and used with a GPU on a personal computer (PC), with recall and precision of 71% and 77%, respectively, with an inference time of 1.5 seconds on a smartphone device. Faramarzi [42] processed the same dataset [33] using different architectures, batch sizes, and optimizers. The CNN model designs employed were YOLO, tiny-YOLOv2, Darknet Neural Networks, YOLOv3, tiny-YOLOv3, and YOLOv4 algorithms, with a mean average precision (mAP) of 45%, or 0.45, and a loss of less than 1.5. Zhang et al. [37] examined the performance of road defect classification using the CrackNet II model with different kernel sizes. The CrackNet II model has 10 layers: four convolution layers, five 1 × 1 convolution layers, and one output layer. The planned CrackNet II includes two significant changes to the original CrackNet. First, CrackNet II no longer uses the feature generator, which delivers the collaborative features of ten developers using a predetermined technique that cannot be developed. As a result, all of the layers in CrackNet II contain learnable parameters that may be adjusted throughout the training process. Second, CrackNet II has a more complex architecture, with more hidden levels but fewer settings. All layers employ the same image width and height to ensure that each pixel's mistake is explicitly taught. For faster convergence, hyperparameters, smaller batch sizes, and a higher learning rate are used first. Later, refining was carried out using a larger batch size and a lower learning rate. CrackNet II obtained 90.20% overall accuracy, 89.06% recall, and 89.62% F-measure on 200 test photos. When compared to the original CrackNet, CrackNet II can detect more fine cracks or hairlines while reducing more local sounds and retaining significantly faster processing performance.

According to Tables 5, there are still various options to improve inference time and FPS, such as using accelerators, adding pruning techniques, post-training quantization, and others [74], [75], [76], [77].

V. CONCLUSION

This study's iYOLOV7-TPE-SS model investigates system performance as mAP, recall, and F1-score improvement utilizing multilevel hyperparameter optimization with a Tree-Structured Parzen Estimator and Space search. Based on the results, the iYOLOV7-TPE-SS model that uses hyperparameter Adam and batch size 16, provides the highest system performance and the lowest loss. The results of the system performances of the iYOLOV7-TPE-SS model are total loss of 0.031, precision of 0.986, recall of 0.970, mAP @0.5 of 0.988, mAP @0.5:0.95 of 0.806, and F1score of 0.978. The iYOLOV7-TPE-SS model is suitable for real-time road damage detection on edge devices due to its high scalability and fast inference time, comparable to YOLOV7-tiny as a lightweight model (0.135 sec and 7.47 FPS on the NVIDIA Jetson Nano). Although the embedded edge NVIDIA AGX Orin device may produce more FPS of 67.034 and has a faster inference time of 0.014 seconds. Our future work in road damage detection development will leverage CNN models installed on edge devices, which enable mobile and real-time data road damage collection. Several recommendations are offered for further research or implementation. First, system performance can be enhanced by integrating various modules into the YOLOV7 framework, such as New Residual Blocks, Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP), and modifications to the Path Aggregation Network. Additionally, optimizing the YOLOV7 model's hyperparameters using techniques like grid search, random search, Bayesian optimization, and TPE can further improve performance. Second, the implementation of CNN models on edge devices for road detection systems can be upgraded by balancing system performance and efficiency through methods such as quantization, pruning, and other optimization techniques. To improve implementation efficiency, strategies like post-training quantization and converting TensorFlow Lite models to TensorRT can be employed to reduce inference time. Finally, the edge device model can be utilized for realtime road defect detection using UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicle) devices.
